# Who is impacted in Tunisia

Research note for the professor’s question: the named personas are design tools. These are the verifiable groups. Ages, jobs, and quotes in the persona cards are not interviews.

Checked 23 September 2026. Where two official-looking numbers disagree, both are kept.

## What Tunisia has, and what this project still must not claim

Tunisia records crashes, runs a large public-transport operator in Greater Tunis, and measures outdoor air. This note did not find a public adaptive traffic-signal network, a scraped intersection-weather-crash table, or an interviewed pedestrian, driver, controller, paramedic, or parent. Absence from these sources is not proof that no such system exists.

Do not say the SUMO runs measured Tunisian deaths, Tunisian air, or lives saved.

## Sources

### Deaths and who dies

WHO country profile, Global status report on road safety 2023, cleared with Tunisia’s national data focal point: [road-safety-2023-tun.pdf](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/country-profiles/road-safety/road-safety-2023-tun.pdf).

- Reported fatalities: 2,273 in 2019.
- Of those reported deaths: 84% male, 16% female.
- WHO estimate for 2021: 2,004 deaths (95% interval 1,786–2,222), 16.3 per 100,000 population.

WHO, Ministry of Health, and Ministry of the Interior investment case, 2025: [Road safety in Tunisia](https://uniatf.who.int/docs/uniatflibraries/default-document-library/road-safety-tunisia-v05-090725.pdf). It quotes ONSR routine statistics for 2023:

- 5,796 crashes, 7,909 injuries, 2,273 fatalities.
- More than 82% of fatalities were male.
- More than 60% were the least protected users: pedestrians 28%, motorcyclists 32%, cyclists 2%.
- Leading recorded causes over several years: careless driving, speeding, failure to give way.
- The 2024 National Road Safety Strategy includes pedestrian and cyclist safety, and accessibility for children, older people, and people with disabilities.
- The case states that only 4.5% of women and 22.5% of men have access to a private car, so women rely more on public transport. That pair of percentages is inside the investment case; the underlying study is cited there as source 26, and was not opened for this note.

A second public account of ONSR does not match the death count. La Presse, 17 January 2025, reports ONSR figures for civil year 2024 and compares them with 2023: [Bilan 2024](https://www.lapresse.tn/2025/01/17/onsr-bilan-des-accidents-de-la-route-en-2024-encore-loin-du-compte/).

- 2024: 5,687 crashes, 1,169 deaths, 7,786 injuries.
- 2023, in that article: 5,796 crashes and 1,216 deaths.
- Tunis governorate in 2024: 953 crashes, 136 deaths, 1,146 injuries, the highest crash count among governorates.
- Inattention 42.11% of crashes. Speeding 14.35% of crashes and about 30% of deaths. Unsafe pedestrian crossing 8.12% of crashes.

The crash total for 2023 matches the investment case (5,796). The death total does not (2,273 versus 1,216). Show both. Do not average them.

INS republishes Interior ministry accident counts: [ins.tn/statistiques/117](http://www.ins.tn/statistiques/117). The 2023 total is 5,796. “Fautes de piétons” is a cause class (444 in 2023), not a count of pedestrians killed.

ONSR’s own site is [onsr.nat.tn](http://www.onsr.nat.tn/onsr/index.php?page=3fr). The annual tables used above were read through the WHO case and the press briefing, not as a downloaded ONSR spreadsheet.

### Bus riders

TRANSTU activity report for 2018, on the operator’s site: [RA-FR-2018](https://www.transtu.tn/uploads/FCK_files/RA-FR-2018_version_def.pdf). Index of reports 2010–2020: [transtu.tn reports](https://www.transtu.tn/fr/0020-rapports-d-activite.html).

- Legal role: transport in Greater Tunis.
- 2018 paying passengers, bus and rail together: 187.4 million, down 1.2% from 2017, mostly because school and university subscriptions fell 6.1%.
- Paying passengers excluding school and university subscriptions: 124.3 million, up 1.4%.
- Bus network described in that report: 231 lines, about 7,126 km, depots including Bab Saadoun, Zahrouni, and Charguia.
- Metro: 6 lines, 61.3 km of double track.

A 2016 report on the same site gives 196.5 million paying passengers. Use the year you cite. Do not call 187.4 million the current ridership.

### Air, not a weather map

ANPE, national ambient-air network: [station network](https://www.anpe.nat.tn/Fr/reseau-des-stations-de-mesure-de-la-qualite-de-leair-ambiant_11_171).

- 30 fixed stations, connected to a centre at the El Mourouj supersite.
- Analysers include nitrogen oxides, particles, carbon monoxide, ozone, and sulphur dioxide, plus weather instruments.
- Values are compared with Tunisian standard NT 106.04.

That is measured air for Tunisia. It is not the SUMO/HBEFA proxy in this repository, and it is not an intersection crash heatmap.

The National Institute of Meteorology also publishes satellite pollution notes, for example September 2024, on [meteo.tn](https://www.meteo.tn/sites/default/files/2024-10/Rapport%20technique%20sur%20la%20pollution%20atmosph%C3%A9rique%20en%20Tunisie%20pour%20le%20mois%20de%20septembre%202024.pdf). Those grids are about 20 km across. They do not locate a school gate.

### Emergency response

The National Civil Protection Office is part of the Ministry of the Interior and runs road-rescue vehicles. A news report of a 10-minute average in 69% of regions was not taken from an official bulletin opened for this note, so it is not used as evidence.

## How to say this in the design-thinking story

The people impacted, on these sources, are: men, who are most of the deaths; pedestrians and motorcyclists, who are a large share of deaths; vehicle occupants, who carry most of the cost; TRANSTU passengers in Greater Tunis, including students; women, who less often have a car; and residents in places ANPE already measures.

The named cards that remain are Amara, David, Chidi, Rosa, Marcus, and Omar. They are research-informed characters standing in for the groups above. Rosa also holds the incident report, so there is no second operator. The school-street air impact stays a measured place, not a named person. The simulation remains a test of the decision rules, not a count of Tunisian victims.
