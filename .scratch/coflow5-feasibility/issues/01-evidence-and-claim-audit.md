# Evidence and claim audit

Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by:

## Question

Which claims, figures, citations, and architectural inferences in the pasted Define/Ideate revision are supported by primary sources, which need narrower wording, and which must be rejected or labelled as proposed?

Audit at minimum: the “33-row verified evidence trail” count; pedestrian wait and walking-speed figures; DfT reliability and waiting multipliers; ALS survival wording; emergency-preemption externalities; SCATS/SCOOT/Surtrac claims; school-road proximity; incident share; Finkelberg communication sensitivity; RESCO/T-REX conclusions; CoLLMLight and AgentSUMO limitations; direct-CO2 reward claim; and every numerical decision-matrix value presented as more than a team judgement.

The answer must distinguish primary evidence, secondary evidence, project assumptions, proposed targets, and unsupported claims. It must not silently harmonise conflicts with the current requirements book.

## Answer

The overall human-centred direction is defensible, but the current documents overstate evidence maturity. Required corrections:

- Replace “33-row verified evidence trail” with **“32-row mixed-source evidence register, audited by source type.”**
- Kingsley reports **12.5%** of students in the studied sample within 250 m (6.2% within 100 m + 6.3% at 100–250 m), not 13.5%; the paper is in the *Journal of Exposure Science & Environmental Epidemiology*, not *IJERPH*.
- Nelson and Bullock supports a case-specific worst arterial increase of about **20–30 s**. The **7.6%** figure belongs to a separate Virginia Route 7 study and must not share that attribution.
- T-REX used **1,400 versus 100 training episodes** for selected methods (14× the episode budget); it did not establish a universal 14× training-cost ratio.
- The ALS study found **7% lower adjusted odds** of survival to discharge per additional minute (aOR 0.93, 95% CI 0.89–0.97) in one retrospective cohort, not a guaranteed seven-percentage-point survival loss.
- Decision-matrix scores/weights, priorities, schedules, thresholds, and acceptance values are **team judgements or proposed targets**, not empirical evidence.
- Several values are secondary-only or need narrow attribution, including assistive-device speed tables, the exact 39% SCOOT figure, and some field preemption summaries.

Every promoted claim must carry one of: verified primary evidence, secondary-only evidence, project assumption, proposed target, or unsupported/overstated.
